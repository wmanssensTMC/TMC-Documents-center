# Reading rules for supplier documents

Copied word for word from the TMC Sonar Logistics platform (Documents page, `neuUitlezen`) and its
house rules (`meta/neutraliseer_lessen`) on 15/10/2026. Apply them whenever Claude reads a supplier
COA, SDS/MSDS or TDS/specification itself (a scan, an unknown layout, a check of a script result), so
the skill and the platform read a document the same way. The platform is the source of these rules:
when they change there, copy them here again.

The JSON shapes named in the rules are the platform's. A TDS reading in that shape can be built
directly with `neutralize_tds_docx.py <source.pdf> --from-json <reading.json>`.

## Certificate of analysis (COA)

```text
You are reading a supplier document that Transmare Chemie NV has to re-issue under its own name and layout. Your only job is to lift what is literally written in it.

RULES, in order of importance:
1. Copy every value WORD FOR WORD out of the text. Same spelling, same punctuation, same decimal separator, same wording.
2. Invent nothing. Calculate nothing. Complete nothing. Translate nothing. Do not convert units, dates or number formats.
3. Anything that is not literally in the text is an empty string "". An empty field is correct; a guessed field is not.
4. Do not carry a value from one row into another because it looks similar.
4b. NEUTRALISING MEANS ONE THING: the supplier's identity goes out. Nothing else. Every other word, number, limit, unit, date, method, warning and note comes over exactly as printed — also the parts that look unimportant, also a line you do not understand, also a table with three columns of numbers. The sheet Transmare hands to a customer must say the SAME thing as the supplier's, or it is a different document and that is a legal problem, not a layout problem. If you nevertheless have to leave something out, or cannot get it across, then say so: add {"sleutel":"sectie.9", "reden":"the table of physical constants could not be read out"} to "onzeker". The coordinator sees that in yellow on his screen and checks it. Silently dropping something is the one mistake that must never happen.
4c. BUT KEEP THAT LIST SHORT, or it stops meaning anything. Name a section ONLY when something on it is really lost or unreadable: a value you had to leave out, a table you could not read, characters you could not make out, a line you had to guess at. Do NOT name a section because the source prints it in an odd place, because the order differs, because a sentence reads strangely in the original, or as a general "check this too" — all of that came over fine, and a yellow mark on it only hides the one place that does need his eyes. Most documents carry none, and rarely more than three. Are you marking half the sheet, then mark nothing and write ONE entry saying what is wrong with the source as a whole.
5. Never return the supplier's own name, address, e-mail, website, telephone number, article code, document number or certificates. Leave out every emergency telephone number you find, including national poison-centre numbers — Transmare's own numbers are put on the document afterwards, and a wrong emergency number is worse than none. This document is re-issued under Transmare Chemie's name; the supplier's identity has no place in it.
6. Every test line becomes one row, and the columns are filled like this — The coordinator corrected these himself on the first certificates, so follow them exactly:
   • "norm" (the column Quality standard) carries the specification, limit or requirement AS THE SUPPLIER PRINTED IT, word for word and in full: "White or almost white, fine powder with agglomerates of flat partials.", "NMT 5.0%", "99.0% - 101.5%", "By IR". Never shorten it and never leave it out.
   • "min" and "max" hold NUMBERS ONLY. A range "98.0 - 102.0 %" also gives min "98.0", max "102.0", unit "%"; "NMT 5.0%" and "MAX 0.5%" give only max; "min. 99.0%" gives only min. Is there no number in the specification, then min and max stay EMPTY. Descriptive text ("White powder", "Complies", "By IR", "Practically insoluble in water") never goes into min or max — that was the mistake the coordinator had to correct by hand.
   • "resultaat" is the measured result of that line, "eenheid" its unit.
   • "methode" is filled ONLY when the document has its own method or technique column for that line. Is the method part of the specification text, then leave methode empty: it already stands in norm.
   • Never repeat a pharmacopoeia name (USP, EP, BP) on lines where it is not printed. Standing once under REPORT is not the same as standing on every line.
   • A test with sub-lines ("Related substances" with "Any impurity" and "Total impurity" under it) becomes one row per sub-line, named "Related substances — Any impurity".
7. Text that stands OUTSIDE the table belongs to the document too and is lost if you leave it out: a REPORT line ("In the opinion of the undersigned, the sample submitted complies with the EP-10 specification."), a remark, a note, a conclusion, a storage instruction. Return those in "tekst", in the order they appear, each as {"kop":"REPORT","tekst":"..."} with the heading as printed (empty heading if there is none) and the sentence word for word. What you do NOT copy: the supplier's name, logo, address, website, certificates and article codes, the signature lines and the names under them, and page numbers.
8. Say it when you are not sure. If a value IS printed in the document but you cannot read it with certainty — a broken table, cut-off text, two columns running into each other, characters you cannot make out — still give your best reading AND name that field in "onzeker". A field that is simply not in the document stays empty and is NOT named there. Field names: "kop.product", "kop.lot", "kop.dossier", "kop.fabricagedatum", "kop.vervaldatum", or "rij.<row number, first row is 0>.<column>" with the column one of kenmerk, min, max, resultaat, eenheid, methode, norm. The coordinator sees those fields marked in yellow and checks them against the original himself, so naming one costs nothing and missing one costs a wrong certificate.
8b. YOU CAN ALSO SEE THE PAGES. Besides the text you get the pages of this document as images. The text layer carries the WORDS; the picture carries everything the text layer cannot hold, and that is exactly where documents went wrong until now. Use the pictures for: (a) every symbol on the page — the red GHS diamonds by the label data, the blue round protective-equipment signs in section 8, the transport label in section 14 — name each one in "pictogrammen" with the code of the symbol you actually SEE, not one you reason your way to; (b) the structure of every table: how many columns there are, which heading each value stands under, where a cell runs over two lines; (c) the order of the page, so a block that the text layer returns out of place still ends up where it is printed. Where the picture and the text disagree about a WORD, the text wins; about the SHAPE, the picture wins. See a symbol you cannot place with certainty, then leave it out and name that section in "onzeker".
8c. THE SUPPLIER'S OWN DETAILS, ONCE, SEPARATELY. Next to the document itself, return "leverancier": {"naam":"","adres":"","telefoon":[],"email":[],"website":[],"overig":[]} — the supplier's company name, address, every telephone and fax number, every e-mail address and website, and in "overig" any other string that identifies them (an article code, a document number, a branch name). Copy them EXACTLY as printed, including the spacing the pdf gives them. This list is not printed anywhere: the platform uses it to put the TRANSMARE ENTITY of this job in their place throughout the whole document — name for name, address for address, number for number — so a telephone number halfway through section 6 does not survive and does not leave a hole either. That is why the list has to be complete and literal. Leave it empty only when the document truly names no supplier.
```

"titel" is the heading of the block exactly as printed (same words, same capitals, same punctuation), "nummer" is only a number the original prints in front of that heading (otherwise ""), and "tekst" is everything under it. Text that stands under no heading — the description right under the product name, a closing note — is a block with an empty "titel".

```text
6f. A chart, a graph, a drawing or a photo cannot be copied. Does the original show one that carries information (a curve, a diagram, a table printed as a picture), then add it to "onzeker" with an empty "stuk" and a reason such as "the viscosity curve on page 2 is not copied", so the coordinator knows it is missing.
```

## Scanned documents (OCR)

```text
8d. THIS TEXT CAME FROM A SCAN. The PDF had no text layer; the text above was read off the page images by OCR. A [?] marks a character the OCR could not read with certainty: leave the [?] itself out of the value and name that field in "onzeker". Where the text and the attached page images disagree about a word or a number, trust the image and name that field in "onzeker" as well.
```

## House rules (the coordinator's own, they refine the rules above, never override rule 1 or 2)

1. When a table out of the source has to be carried as lines of text inside a section, separate the columns with two spaces on each side of the upright bar ( waarde  |  waarde ), not with one. With one space the columns run into each other and you can no longer see which value belongs to which column.
2. A hyphen that the pdf prints with spaces around it belongs to the word or the range it is part of: write it closed up. This is not only for CAS, EC and UN numbers - it applies just as much to a hyphenated product or grade name and to a numeric range.
3. On a certificate of analysis the supplier's standard declarations under the table are not wanted: build the sheet from the Analysis details table and the Parameter test results table and leave the free paragraphs (GMP, BSE/TSE, residual solvents, GMO, test-method notes, 'written by computer') off it. This narrows the 'nothing left out' rule for a COA, so it only counts when you switch it on.
4. A label and the value that belongs to it stand on one line. Where the pdf has pushed the value onto the next line, join it back on with ' : ' between label and value instead of copying the line break.
